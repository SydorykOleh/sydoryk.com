Hi!

Your project for smartcarmanuals.com caught my eye, and I'd love to help you build out this 3D library. A highly optimized, interactive collection of automotive parts is a fantastic use case for web 3D. I'm very comfortable with hard-surface modeling, creating exploded/cutaway views, and optimizing assets for web delivery under strict size limits.

Below are the details on how I would approach the pilot batch, my software pipeline, and some relevant examples of my past work.

### 1. Software Stack & Renderer
**Software Stack:** Houdini and its procedural pipeline, with Nuke for image processing.
**Renderer:** Karma

*Why it fits technical illustration work:* Houdini's procedural architecture is incredibly powerful for building scalable, iterative assets like a library of 100+ car parts. It allows me to maintain precise control over geometry and easily update non-destructive setups if the visual standard changes. For final presentation, I use Nuke to handle compositing and image processing, ensuring clean, artifact-free renders with perfect studio lighting that match the technical, Haynes-manual aesthetic you're looking for.

### 2. Portfolio Examples
My portfolio project closest to mechanical assemblies is my **Industrial Equipment** visualization. It demonstrates clean hard-surface modeling and realistic material representation (metals, plastics, paint) under studio lighting. 
I have also included my **Microphone Configurator** which showcases high-quality materials and lighting, my **Old Microphone** detailed visualization, and a simple real-time interactive part to demonstrate my web 3D capabilities.
<p><em>Note: Additional high-resolution rendering examples are attached directly in the Upwork proposal.</em></p>

### 3. Fixed Fee Per Part
For the complete deliverable package (Hero render, Exploded view, Cross-section, 3D source file, and Web-ready GLB export), my fixed fee is **$200 per part**. This easily fits within your pilot budget and covers modeling from reference, precise texturing, rendering, and revision rounds.

### 4. Realistic Pilot Cadence
For the initial 5-part pilot batch, a realistic timeline is **2 to 3 weeks**. The first few parts always require a bit more time to properly establish the workflow, set up the lighting rigs, and define the exact material library (metals, rubber, plastics) so everything looks perfectly consistent. Once that workflow is established, delivery will be much faster. During Phase 2, the main limiting factor will just be the 3D modeling time itself.

### 5. Web-Ready GLB/GLTF Export Experience
While I typically specialize in delivering high-quality, pre-rendered images and animations, I am very familiar with the realtime pipeline. There is no problem for me to optimize the geometry and bake the textures down to meet your under-2MB GLB delivery requirement.
